## 1. The question, and the straight answer

The straight answer, given first because the question had a premise in it. WebAssembly was never the lever, and nobody had said the engine cannot beat Excel. It already does, on one thread against Excel's twenty, on whole recalculations of every fixture the floors hold and on most edits (§2.1). What a WebAssembly-first storage schema cannot do is move those floors, because the wins come from the language's plan and not from the bytecode: the same Rust runs a quarter to a half faster native than in the browser on every frame the floors time (*measurement*, §2.2). The assessment of `Mitochondrion/` of 2026-10-22 had said as much of a storage schema (*shelf*: the Delta memory of that day; `Frazaro/docs/BETA_REARVIEW.md`, `KERNEL.49`'s record, whose decision 4 shipped the one idea that schema tested). (*measurement* and *shelf*)

And speed is not what makes Excel a pocket calculator. A pocket calculator has buttons and a display. It has no source, no history, no proof and no frame rate. Excel is the largest pocket calculator ever built in exactly that sense, and the stack already has all four missing things in principle: the source is the cartridge and the sentences, the history is the replay, the proof is the determinism and the goldens, and the frame rate is the engine's. What the stack lacks is the application wrapped around them, and that application is split across two repositories whose pages have never met. Alonzo draws a grid and opens no workbook, since the reader is `frazaro-core`'s and the engine never depends on the core (*shelf*: `AD-1`). Frazaro's page opens workbooks and draws no grid, since the viewport's sentence-linking half is unbuilt (*shelf*: Frazaro's `KERNEL.5`, its roadmap entry; `Frazaro/web/README.md`, "nothing on the page calls it yet"). (*shelf* throughout; the reading *recommendation*)

### 2.3 The reading

- **Why it wins.** A column of identical formulas is one shape evaluated as a vector, nothing unchanged is recomputed, and identical shapes share one plan (*shelf*: `Frazaro/web/CALLOSUM.md` §12.6's three advantages; `KERNEL.20`, `KERNEL.43`, `KERNEL.49`). Those are properties of the language. They would be faster still compiled native, and they are what a storage schema cannot supply.
- **Why a WebAssembly-first store does not help.** Life's rule reads about 21 twin cells per cell at roughly 2 ns a read (*measurement*, the Mitochondrion assessment of 2026-10-22, over `life-x16`). Any boundary in that path, a host call among them, costs microseconds (*shelf*: `ENGINE.19`'s record). WebAssembly is the distribution, never the speed.
- **Where it loses.** Edits into irregular graphs, where the netlist's edit costs more than its whole frame and Excel's single thread wins (§2.1). One thread against twenty, since Excel's engine is C++ and has split its calculation chain across every core since 2007 (*exhibit*, §9). And coverage: 132 of about 500 functions, about an eighth of Excel the application by the sitting of 2026-10-22 (*shelf*: `REARVIEW.md`, THE SITTINGS, that date).
- **The missing number.** No real multi-sheet financial model has been run as a rate-zero cartridge beside Excel. Buffet is the nearest shape. The algebra named the gap the first evening, "a fourth benchmark that is a model, not a game" (*shelf*: `docs/ALGEBRA.md` §3, correction 5), and the gap assessment of 2026-10-18 put it first; it is minted now (§4).

### 2.4 The headroom

Each a *prediction* until measured, in the order of its size:

- **Threads as a device.** The frame's width at each level is the parallelism on offer (*shelf*: `CHARTER.md` §4, the frame), which the machine has never used. The price is cross-origin isolation for the shared memory, which a page opened from disk cannot have and a static host such as GitHub Pages cannot grant by header, so the route is a service worker that answers the headers or a host that can (*exhibit*, §9: the service-worker trick).
- **The shape compiler.** The charter's third backend, VLA to WebAssembly, held bit for bit to the machine's step: two to five times on a dense regular frame, nothing on an idle one or on Buffet's (*shelf*: `CHARTER.md` §4 rule 2; the Delta memory of the Mitochondrion assessment).
- **Bits through abstract interpretation.** Prove Life's cells always 0 or 1, hold a cell as a bit and step with bitwise adders: two to three orders of magnitude on Life-shaped cells (*shelf*: `docs/ALGEBRA.md` §10.5).
- **The irregular edit path.** The one place Excel still wins (§2.1), which deserves the netlist as its fixture.

## 3. What a pocket calculator lacks

### 3.1 The axis

The axis to change is not features. Javelin and Improv were better spreadsheets and lost to the grid (*shelf*: `Frazaro/docs/HORIZON.md` §10.2). The house's own documents say the line already: the spreadsheet is the only programming medium on Earth whose programs have no source code (*shelf*: `web/CALLOSUM.md` §12.1). A pocket calculator cannot be diffed, replayed, tested, signed or run at sixty frames a second, and neither can Excel. The stack's four properties are the axis, and every tool below is a tool for one of them. (*recommendation*)

### 3.2 The demo in ten seconds

Graham's question of the first evening was what a visitor sees in ten seconds (*shelf*: `docs/ALGEBRA.md` §2.4). The demo that makes the point, each step with its state on 2026-10-25:

2. You drag time backwards and open the previous frame. The twins are viewable; scrubbing is not built (minted, §4).
3. You click a cell and its inputs light up. Not built on this page; the cone walk lives in the core's file reader (`core/src/reflect/cone.rs`), which the engine may not depend on (minted, §4).

### 3.4 The CLI

The palette is already a REPL whose language is VLA plus commands: a text beginning with a parenthesis is a row written as a person's edit, custom commands have steps, and Ctrl+Up brings back what was run (*shelf*: `ENGINE.23`). What makes it a CLI in the owner's own sense, short forms with capture variables (*shelf*: the Frazaro memory of the owner's design values, 2026-09-19):

- **Arguments and captures.** A command takes named arguments and its steps use them, its expansion shown as the pass's rule requires. The spec names this as not in version 5 (*shelf*: `SPEC.md` §12).
- **A transcript.** A command's answer lands in a Transcript sheet, so output is a cell.
- **Composition.** A command's step runs another command with arguments; the Lisp way, no new syntax.
- **English.** The sentence typed in the palette (`ENGINE.29`) turns the REPL into Frazaro's console window with a grid under it.
- **The OS half.** Frazaro has a door of twelve commands; this repository has cargo examples. A native `alonzo` binary is the arcade's command line and the headless verifier proof of play wants.

One room, three doors, Excel's console, the page's palette and the shell, with one grammar across them (*shelf*: `Frazaro/docs/HORIZON.md` §11.4). (*recommendation*)

### 3.5 The arcade

The engine already does what a fantasy console does: stated limits, a cartridge as the unit, replays, a picker, a kit (*shelf*: `SPEC.md` §10; `CHARTER.md` §5). What PICO-8 and TIC-80 have that it lacked on 2026-10-25 (*exhibits*, §9; *shelf* for the engine's side):

- **A save.** The host counts the save cell's edge and writes nothing (`web/host.js`); `ENGINE.5` is filed with the save's shape as its one decision. A console without save is a toy by its own measure.
- **Export in one click.** A game vendors the page through a build script (`ENGINE.15`); the player itself offers nothing.
- **Sprites and scroll.** The composite projection, named the first evening as the answer to the gather cost (*shelf*: `docs/ALGEBRA.md` §3, correction 3), kept out of every version because every cartridge drew on a grid of squares.
- **Touch and a gamepad.** The page has neither (*shelf*: `web/README.md`, *Not in this version*; `SPEC.md` §12).
- **Proof of play and a registry.** The signed replay is filed (`ENGINE.10`); the arcade repository is filed (`REPO.5`); a registry is a repository one clones and never fetches (*shelf*: `web/CALLOSUM.md` §14.1).

The arcade's three strategic jobs: the social hypothesis counted in forks (*shelf*: `CHARTER.md` §6), the on-ramp for authors, and the benchmark a stranger believes, since nobody doubts a game running at sixty. (*recommendation*)

## 6. The moonshots for 2030

### 6.1 The frame

By 2028 the platform shift the landlord is riding has happened: agents do corporate work. Every counter-bet in the house's documents wins on the crisis a shift produces (*shelf*: `HORIZON.md` §10.2), and the crisis here is already named, plausible text is not a record (*shelf*: `HORIZON.md` §6). The scarce thing in a world of agents is a surface where an agent's work is verifiable by the person who signs it. That surface exists only in this stack, and nearly every moonshot below is a consequence of that one fact. (*recommendation*)

### 6.2 The twelve

1. **The agent's terrarium.** A cartridge imports nothing, so it cannot phone home. Every act in it is a row, every row is checked, refused or run, every effect is a cell, and the replay replays the agent exactly. Give agents the books through that surface and nothing else, and the CFO lets them touch the books, because a control refuses the act before it lands and the record names the key. "The model proposes, the record disposes" stops being a boundary in one tool and becomes the operating system of agentic finance. Lineage: `LE.7`, the drafting bridge; `HORIZON.md` §6; Miller's object capabilities (*exhibit*, §9). *Engineering*, since the sandbox exists; the adoption is *fiction*, social. The needle that moves furthest by 2030.

2. **The internal tool as a cartridge.** Buffet is a seven-view application in four thousand lines of VLA with a replay as its audit log (*measurement*: `wc -l` over `Buffet/src/`, 2026-10-25). Every department's internal tool, the approvals tracker, the vendor onboarding form, the headcount plan, is a spreadsheet with a thin UI, and today it costs a subscription and a developer. As a cartridge it is a text file with views, typed input rows (`SPEC.5`'s `(work ...)` sheets), commands, per-range capabilities (the shape `ENGINE.12` names) and a signed record. Lineage: HyperCard, Access, Airtable (*exhibits*, §9). *Engineering*; adoption *fiction*. The needle: the back-office tooling market, and the end of the shadow database.

3. **One grid, all time.** Every workbook of a company in one machine, one dependency graph, cross-book references ordinary, content-addressed shapes so that four thousand models collapse to a few hundred (*shelf*: `web/CALLOSUM.md` §4, item 3), the cone across the estate in microseconds, and the frame scrubber over all of it: "the company at 2029-03-31 16:41", every number derivable, a new control replayed over a decade of history before it is adopted (*shelf*: `HORIZON.md` §9.2 items 6 and 7). *Derived*: three hundred million formula cells at about 20 bytes a cell is six gigabytes before the plan's sharing. Lineage: differential dataflow (*shelf*: `docs/ALGEBRA.md` §10.5). *Engineering*, large. The needle: the finance data warehouse and the ERP's reporting layer become one live fixpoint.

4. **Exact money.** Abstract interpretation picks a column's representation: whole cents as integers, flags as bits, dates as days, ratios as rationals, floating point only where exactness cannot be proven. The close foots to the cent, a tenth plus two tenths is three tenths, and the penny-difference ritual that opens every reconciliation ends. The oracle policy the algebra's first crack asked for gets written: the language's own functions exact, Excel's agreement with its cached values at a stated tolerance (*shelf*: `docs/ALGEBRA.md` §7; `KERNEL.8`'s fifteen-digit decision). Lineage: decimal floating point in IEEE 754-2008, Julia's rationals (*exhibits*, §9); the owner's decimal item in Buffet's asks table (*shelf*). *Math exists*, *engineering*; the policy the owner's. Small and big at once.

5. **The explorable model.** With an act reaching the painted frame in under a millisecond on a board of 800,000 cells (§2.2), every assumption can be a slider at a kilohertz and every output a live chart, the fiscal year scrubbed under the hand while the board watches the model move. Bret Victor's explorable explanation with a proof under it and a replay behind it. Lineage: Victor's essays of 2011; Sutherland's Sketchpad (*exhibits*, §9). *Engineering*; the latency exists. The needle: how executives relate to a model, and the end of the static deck.

6. **The budget as a multiplayer game.** The annual plan takes months of email because it is a distributed edit with no merge. As a room it is seats, a shared seed, a replay, branches merged by relation (`KERNEL.21`) and the board's what-if a fork; Buffet is the single-player proof that a fund's year is playable. Lineage: lockstep's fifteen hundred archers (*shelf*: `docs/ALGEBRA.md` §10.9); war-gaming. *Engineering* behind the network gate, and by exchanged replays without any network today. The needle: planning cycles of months become sessions with a record.

7. **Regulation as a library.** A regulator publishes its rules as a library of sentences, a lease standard, a liquidity ratio, a modeling standard; a filing is a replay plus the library; the regulator re-runs it. Compliance becomes a green column instead of a certificate, and the horizon's note on XBRL, the standard that shipped the number without the proof, is answered (*shelf*: `HORIZON.md` §5 item 2; `web/CALLOSUM.md` §5 item 5). Lineage: Catala (*exhibit*, §9). *Engineering*, small; adoption *fiction*. The needle, if adopted, is the compliance industry.

8. **The SQLite of calculation.** A module of under two megabytes with no imports (*measurement*: 1,819,745 bytes at the seventeenth baseline) that computes any declared model deterministically, embedded in every invoice system, dashboard, game and device the way SQLite sits in every phone (*exhibit*, §9). ## 7. VLA in 2030: the language the moonshots need

*The third sitting. The owner's question, in the owner's words: VLA "is currently a hodge-podge of empirical spreadsheet helper functions derived from Excel's VBA gaps"; peek into the distant future and report what VLA should and will look like in 2030; hundreds of cartridges, thousands of templates and millions of lines later, what do they teach, and where are the biggest gaps the author of 2030 would travel back to 2026 to close, "macros, functors, features, and forms which would make their life easier to author spreadsheet templates, game cartridges, and other unforeseen modalities of Lisp-oriented spreadsheet toolings". The session read the language as both implementations hold it on 2026-10-25 before answering: the head table, the expander's own account of itself, the prelude, the kit, Buffet's kitchen, the five games' own macros, the expander's refusals by name, and the scoping of `VLA.6` written the same day.*

### 7.1 What VLA is today, honestly

The owner's word is fair, and the measurement says why. VLA is four notations wearing one syntax, and three of them were born from Excel's gaps (*measurement*, 2026-10-25, over `Frazaro/vla-lang/data/headtable.vla`, `Frazaro/scripts/prelude.vla`, `cartridges/common.vla`, `Buffet/src/kitchen.vla` and the five games' sources):

- **The statement language.** The head table's sixty-six forms are VBA's statements with Lisp's parentheses: `sub`, `function`, `type`, `enum`, `dim`, `const`, `for`, `for-each`, `select`, `with`, `goto`, `on-error`, `raw`, `debug-print`, and the operators. The prelude's forty-seven macros are VBA's conveniences, `with-screen-off`, `with-no-alerts`, `with-fast-excel`, `with-protected-sheet`, `inc!`, `when-let`, `try-else`, and a list library written as continuation-passing walks, `length-onto`, `reverse-onto`, `append-onto`, `quote-map-onto`, because the expander had no loop and no return value. This is the language's first life, the compiler from sentences to the add-in, and it is the hodge-podge the owner names. It is one backend's dialect (*shelf*: `SD-1`, VBA a backend and not the language), and no cartridge author ever meets it.
- **The formula language.** Excel's own, 132 functions computed by `vla-lang` and the rest labelled, with `formula-expand` writing an expression as a formula's text in Excel's spelling and reading it back (*shelf*: `VLA.4`, `KERNEL.8`), `lambda` and `deflambda` in the formula dialect, and `LET` and `LAMBDA` parity minted (`KERNEL.29`). This is the language the machine runs, and it is Excel's by design, since the oracle is Excel's saved values.
- **The template language.** `defmacro` as pure substitution, the quasiquote bundle, `quote-if`, the list primitives, the ten of the expand-time object model (`&expand`, `address-expand`, `column-letters-expand`, `characters-expand`, whole-number arithmetic), `each` and `each-index` since `VLA.5`, `assoc-expand`, and since `VLA.6`, in flight on 2026-10-25, `define`, a name at expand time. No gensym, no eval, a chain of at most 5,000 applications, includes 16 deep, about a hundred and forty refusals by name (*shelf*: `SPEC.md` §7.8; `Frazaro/vla-lang/src/expand.rs`'s head; `vla-lang/data/messages.vla`). This is where every author of a cartridge or a template lives: the kit's 42 macros, Buffet's 22, Kairos's 3, Sand's 6, Naught's 21, Superposition's 31 and Seiyaku's 32 are all written in it.
- **The rows.** The record's notation, `cell`, `formula`, `format`, `style`, `look`, `column`, `row`, `sheet`, `name`, and the device sheets' layouts: the treaty's rows, what the pass writes and the load takes, the waist of the hourglass (*shelf*: `SPEC.md` §7.3; the Alonzo memory of the architecture as an hourglass, 2026-10-11).

Above the four sits a fifth, the sentences, forty phrasebooks' worth one day, each compiling to the first three (*shelf*: `Frazaro/docs/HORIZON.md` §11). The first three names in the owner's question, macros, functors and forms, all live in the third notation, and that is where the gaps are.

### 7.2 What the lines teach

Eleven cartridges, Buffet's four thousand lines and a phrasebook of 240 rules are not a million lines, but the regularities below already hold in every one of them, and the sitting predicts they hold at a million (*shelf* for each observation's source; *prediction* for the extrapolation):

1. **Layout is the cost, not logic.** Buffet's own record describes its sheets by address, "the settings `B1:B6`, the draw `B8:B21`, the buttons `A23:G28`, the workings `B30:B41`" (*shelf*: `Delta/CLAUDE.md`, Buffet's layout), and Kairos cites `State!B12` 169 times where its author thinks "stop" (*shelf*: `VLA.6`'s scoping). Moving a block means rewriting every address in a source, since a reference inside `formula-expand` is a symbol the author typed. Excel adjusts a moved formula; VLA source does not.
2. **The walks are the pain.** Fifteen of the kit's forty-two macros are walks, four of them stacked for one picture (*shelf*: `KIT.6`'s record), the prelude's list library is `-onto` continuation passing, and the pass hands a parameter on as `(then args...)` because a macro's call is no argument of a primitive (*shelf*: `SPEC.md` §7.8, `ENGINE.9`'s note). `VLA.5`'s `each` retires half of them; the other half wants a value returned.
3. **Nesting kills, helpers heal.** Kairos's `board` at 99 lines and depth 13, its body written twice under one `quote-if`; Superposition's `field-rule` with ten parameters (*shelf*: the sitting of 2026-10-22). The owner's rule, three to five variables a form and every unit named, is the lint's (`VLA.7`, `REPO.7`).
4. **Two kinds of helper want to be one.** A verb writes a row and an expression macro returns a form; a verb inside a verb's piece is refused by name (*shelf*: `vla-formula-expand-nested`, `KIT.5`'s record). Authors write the same test twice, once as each kind.
5. **Every game is a few sheet shapes.** A board, a state sheet with phases (Kairos's thirty-one cells in four), a tally, a mind of candidates against heuristics (`KIT.4`), a ring ledger written through the Write sheet (Buffet's), a book of content (Seiyaku's, Buffet's content sheets), a commands table, a camera, a lesson. Hundreds of cartridges will not invent a hundred shapes; they will reuse a dozen with parameters, which is what a functor is for.
6. **The frame problem is solved by values, and the state machine recurs.** The Write sheet carries values across frames (*shelf*: `SPEC.md` §3.9, `docs/ALGEBRA.md` §2.3), and every game writes a phase cell and tests it everywhere. A state machine is a shape, not a formula.
7. **Content wants its own files.** Buffet's `strings` reads a content sheet from a file of words alone; Seiyaku's book is a block of its source between markers (*shelf*: Buffet's `kitchen.vla`; `CART.14`). Data and code separate on their own, and a second language's words follow.
8. **The cost rules are folklore.** A formula that changes every frame in a board cell, a term reading its own column, a class of values as one comparison: six rules an author must know (*shelf*: `SPEC.md` §2's note of 2026-10-11; Kairos's header). The census already counts shapes and widths; nothing tells the author at the pass.
9. **Tests want to live beside the macro.** The phrasebook's every rule carries a `test:`; a `defmacro` carries a docstring and nothing else, and its proof is a game's replay three files away (*shelf*: `Frazaro/scripts/polyglotta/english.vla`; `engine/src/pass.rs`'s kit test).
10. **Names are the real request.** The kit builds a reference's twin by a character walk because no form gives it; `VLA.6` answers with `define` and `last` (*shelf*: its scoping). Every other lesson above is a name's absence somewhere: for a place, for a shape, for a module, for a test.

### 7.3 The gaps, ranked, each with its 2030 form

*The author of 2030's requests, in the order the sitting would grant them. Each names what stands, the form that fills it, its lineage and its mark; the forms are shapes and not grammar, as the algebra's sentences are, so that no mechanism precedes the need that asks for it. Nothing here is minted.*

1. **Names for every cell, range and device** (`VLA.6`, in flight). `(define stop State!B12)`, `(last stop)`, `names` writing a sheet's labels and defines from one list, `devices` naming every device cell from the spec's own labels. The 2030 form goes one step further: a source never cites an address except in the macro that lays a block out, and a reference the author did not name is a lint finding. *Engineering*; the first slice is a Frazaro item under construction.

2. **The fill's own place as a word.** Life's rule reads `Screen.last!A1` at `B2` to mean "my upper-left neighbor last frame", and the author does the relative arithmetic by hand for the fill's first cell. The 2030 form binds a context inside a verb's expression: `self`, `(above self)`, `(within 1 self)`, `(neighbors 8)`, written by the verb that knows its range's anchor into the relative references the record wants. It is a special variable in Lisp's sense, bound by the enclosing verb and read by the expression macros inside it (*exhibit*: Common Lisp's special variables, §9). *Engineering*, days over the formula syntax; the kit's `interior` already computes the anchor it would need.

3. **Verbs as expressions: the two kinds of helper made one.** A verb at the top level writes its row; the same verb inside another's piece yields the formula fragment it would have written, so `(rule r (count Input!B16))` composes where today it is refused by name. *Engineering*; the formula syntax already expands its argument before writing (*shelf*: `VLA.4`), so the change is where a verb's row is made.

4. **Named pieces with defaults.** The arity smell, `field-rule` at ten parameters and `put-from` at seven, is the absence of keyword arguments. The 2030 form: `(defmacro (rule range (formula f) (sheet s "Screen") (look l none)) ...)`, each piece named, each with a default, a caller supplying what differs, which is what `SD-4` already demands of a library's slots (*shelf*: `HORIZON.md` §12.9) and what `assoc-expand` began (*shelf*: `VLA.5`; `KIT.6`'s second slice). Lineage: Common Lisp's `&key` (*exhibit*, §9). *Engineering*, small.

5. **Expand-time functions with values, and `let`.** The expander is already a total, fuel-bounded functional language over forms: it loops (`each`), it branches (`quote-if`), it recurses (a macro's self-expansion to 5,000), it computes (the ten primitives). What it lacks is the one thing that makes such a language composable, a function that returns a value a primitive can take: `(defexpand (grid-address r c) (&expand (column-letters-expand c) r))`, callable where today only data may stand, and `(let-expand ((rows 5) (cols 7)) ...)` binding a value for one form. This retires every `-onto` and `-from` walk, the "hand it on" idiom and the quasiquote deferral trap at once. It does not breach the wall: no symbol is invented (no `GENSYM`), no text becomes code (no `eval`), the fuel stays, and the result is rows a person reads. It does amend one sentence the expander says of itself, "no compile-time computation beyond the expand-time primitives, by design" (*shelf*: `Frazaro/vla-lang/src/expand.rs`'s head), and that amendment is the owner's. Lineage: Racket's phases, computation at expand time separated from run time by a stage, never by a sandbox (*exhibit*, §9: Flatt 2002). *Engineering*, weeks in both implementations; the largest single gift to the author of 2030, and the one decision in this list.

6. **Hygiene by design, not by convention.** Three traps are rules an author must remember: a parameter replaces a row's head as well as an expression (*shelf*: the kit's header, "a parameter never takes the name of a row it writes"); a later `defmacro` silently replaces an earlier one, which `ENGINE.9` turned into its parameter mechanism (*shelf*: `SPEC.md` §7.8, the size ladder's note); and a helper's free symbol is written as a name (*shelf*: `KIT.5`'s Found). The 2030 form: a parameter is replaced only where it stands as an expression, never as a head, which `VLA.6`'s scoping found the walk can do by passing heads by; a redefinition is refused by name unless the source says `(parameter board-size 320 200)` and a door overrides that form alone; an unbound symbol inside `formula-expand` that spells no reference is a refusal, not a name. Lineage: Kohlbecker's hygienic expansion and `syntax-rules`, substitution by structure (*exhibits*, §9). *Engineering*; half of it is `VLA.6`'s.

7. **Modules, `use` and the pin.** An include splices text and names collide in silence; the kit's names are global. The 2030 form: `(module kit ...)` declaring what it exports, `(use kit (only rule rules edge))` with qualified names where two kits meet, the include pinned by its SHA-256 (`REPO.9`), `describe` listing the macros loaded with their docstrings, and a library's expansion visible in the cartridge as the horizon asks of sentence libraries (*shelf*: `HORIZON.md` §12.9, visible expansion; `web/CALLOSUM.md` §14.1). Lineage: Scheme's libraries (R6RS), TeX's packages (*exhibits*, §9). *Engineering*.

8. **Functors: a module that takes a module.** The owner's word, and the shape the games already have by hand: `CART.6`'s Mind generalized at `CART.7` is a module parameterized by its heuristics (*shelf*: `KIT.4`); Buffet's `strings` family is a content module parameterized by a file and its fields; `board-size` handed on is a cartridge parameterized by a door. The 2030 form: `(module (ledger fields writer) ...)` instantiated by `(instance trades (ledger (price count side) put))`, a dozen such shapes in the kit, a board, a state machine, a tally, a mind, a ring ledger, a book, a form, a view, a commands table, a lesson, each taking what differs, so that a cartridge is mostly instances and its rules. Lineage: ML's functors (*exhibit*, §9). *Engineering*, over 5 and 7; the kit's ratchet decides which shapes enter (*shelf*: `ROADMAP.md`, "The order, and why", the note of 2026-10-11).

9. **A layout algebra, and the expander's shadow of what it wrote.** Lesson 1's cost has one fix: geometry as a value. `(block Settings (rows 6) (at Proctor B1))`, `(below Settings 2)`, `(beside ...)`, a block's cells reachable as names, addresses computed and never typed, so that moving a block moves every reference. It needs the expander to keep a shadow of the rows written so far, `(extent "Ledger")` and `(next-row "Ledger")`, which is a two-pass expansion, layout then references, deterministic and in written order. Lineage: TeX's boxes and glue; Cassowary for the version that solves constraints, which the algebra already names (*shelf*: `docs/ALGEBRA.md` §6.3). *Engineering*; the stacking half days, the solving half the algebra's rung 8.

### 7.4 A source of 2030, as a sketch

```text

  => (formula "Budget" "B4:M4" "=B3+B1-B2"))          ; 10: the proof beside the macro

(cartridge "lights" (spec 9) (rate 0) (settle 2) (license "0BSD"))

(sheet Board (grid 5 5) (kind flag) (game))          ; 8: a board shape, instanced
```
