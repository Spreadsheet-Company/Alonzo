## The standing decisions

The charter's seven rules, numbered so an item can cite them. The text is the charter's (§4), and an amendment is made there first.

- **`AD-1`. VLA's seams are the interface, and Frazaro is a door.** `alonzo` consumes `vla-lang` and never `frazaro-core`. A game's dependency tree holds the language crate and the engine crate and not the Frazaro core, checked by `cargo tree` in every game's CI. Frazaro's web door may depend on Alonzo; its crates, its command-line door, its add-in and its treaty never do.
- **`AD-2`. Every early brick is also a Frazaro item, laid where it is first needed.** The ID is Frazaro's and stays in its registers; the code lands on the side of the line `AD-4` draws.
- **`AD-3`. Nothing Frazaro rents is rebuilt until Frazaro has users on the rented version.** The measurement that says the rent is the bottleneck is named before the rebuild begins.
- **`AD-4`. Frazaro has no clock; Alonzo is the clock.** What can be a golden is VLA's or Frazaro's; what can only be measured is Alonzo's. The check on each side is the import section: zero there, an allowlist here.
- **`AD-5`. The engine's imports are the host's functions, named by hand and held to a list; the engine crate depends on the language crate and nothing else.** Each import, when one is needed, an `extern "C"` function the host shim provides, declared one by one; no generated glue, no second dependency; the first specification needs none, every device being a sheet read and written through the exports (`SPEC.md` §8.2). Adopted by the owner 2026-10-08 at the scoping of `REPO.1`; its second sentence amended the same day at the approval of `SPEC.1`.
- **`AD-6`. Everything the engine holds is a cell of a named sheet, viewable through the one view call and, except the previous frame's twins and the plane, writable through the one write call; a device is a sheet's layout in `SPEC.md`, and no device holds state that is not a cell.** Reflection over the whole stack, sanctified: the Keys sheet, the Palette, the Clock and the `.last` twins are its first fruits, and every such act is a row in the one notation, logged by the replay. Adopted by the owner 2026-10-08 at the approval of `SPEC.1`.
- **`AD-7`. The step computes values and never formulas; a formula enters the grid by `load` or by a `write` made on a person's act, and the engine's own effects write values.** Eval by a person, the REPL, kept; eval by the program refused, as the `GENSYM` veto's `eval` clause already has it; the grid writes the grid in data through the Write sheet (`SPEC.md` §3.9); held by a test, the formula cells equal before and after a step. Adopted by the owner 2026-10-08 at the approval of `SPEC.2`, the charter amended first.

Frazaro's standing decisions that cross the road are cited by their own numbers and never re-minted: `SD-13` (no outbound call, ever), `SD-18` (VBA the reference for the language), `SD-23` (one model, many projections; the interface's own state), `SD-34` (every engine interruptible, its progress a sentence), the owner's veto of `GENSYM`, which carries `eval` by its own words (`AD-7`), and the conformance treaty.

## The order, and why

Each item is what makes the next cheap, the instrument before the operation: `REPO.2` before the first import, `SPEC.1` before the first call into the language, `SPEC.2` before the four calls are written against the page, `CART.1` before any accelerator. An item that waits on Frazaro names the Frazaro item and never re-mints it. Two tracks run in parallel until the loop: the repository and the specification need nothing from Frazaro, and the renderer needs only the view record, which exists as goldens; the loop and everything after it need the `vla-lang` cut, Frazaro's work before `KERNEL.7`, which `SPEC.1` tells what to expose. The frame-rate floor needs `KERNEL.7`'s first functions. The games after the first are repositories of their own and leave this file when they are founded. The reasoning and the fan-out table are in `REARVIEW.md` under the sittings of 2026-10-08.

## 🔧 THE ENGINE

- 🟡 **`KERNEL.5` — the viewport, laid here: the Screen device over the view record.** A canvas renderer of one window of a grid, scroll, select, resize columns, freeze headers, crisp at the machine's pixel ratio. It consumes the view record as data, the view goldens of `Frazaro/scripts/view/` vendored as fixtures until `vla-lang` publishes the call that returns the record live. The sentence-linking half of `KERNEL.5` (`SD-20`, `SD-24`, `SD-25`, `SD-28`) is the first game's chrome and stays in Frazaro's page, which consumes this renderer. *Depends on:* `KERNEL.3`'s numbers, measured 2026-10-08 (`CHARTER.md` §7: canvas, subject to the crispness check by eye). *Oracle:* the benchmark's own method as floors in `tools/check_render_floors.ps1`, scroll p95 under one and a half display intervals and max under two, first paint of the normal window under 100 ms, on the 10,000-line record; and the drawn window equal to the record cell for cell. *Closed by:* Frazaro's ledger, the ID being Frazaro's. *Scoped:* 2026-10-08, thirteen decisions, all approved. **Built and owner-tested 2026-10-08, committed at the owner's word; the entry leaves when Frazaro's ledger closes it:** `web/viewport.js` and its page, the seven fixtures pinned, four checks with their controls, the oracle green under headless Chrome and Edge at two ratios, the floors' first baseline the owner's run in Chrome in fullscreen (step p95 0.9 ms at the normal window and 1.4 at full screen, against 24.9; the record is in `REARVIEW.md`). `~weeks`
- ⬜ **`ENGINE.1` — the host shim and the loop.** The JavaScript that instantiates the module with exactly `REPO.2`'s imports and nothing else; the animation-frame loop with a fixed timestep and the frame number written into the Clock sheet; `SD-34`'s budget, a step that yields and reports how far it got, so the grid never freezes; pipelining, the draw of one frame beside the step of the next, when a measurement wants it. *Depends on:* `SPEC.1`; the `vla-lang` cut with the handle and the four calls, Frazaro's work before `KERNEL.7`. `~days` after the cut
- ⬜ **`ENGINE.2` — the frame path.** The Screen sheet read out of the module's memory as a typed array of palette indices and blitted whole, never printed; held equal to the view record of the same window by a test, the record being the oracle (`CHARTER.md` §3). *Depends on:* `KERNEL.5`, `ENGINE.1`. `~days`
- ⬜ **`ENGINE.3` — the Input and Clock devices.** Keys and the mouse, position and buttons, written into the Input sheet's cells each frame before the step; the frame number in the Clock sheet; nothing else is a clock. *Depends on:* `SPEC.1`, `ENGINE.1`. `~days`
- ⬜ **`ENGINE.4` — the Audio device.** The Web Audio graph's own nodes, an oscillator and a gain per voice, driven from the Audio sheet's cells read after the step; no synthesizer of its own (`CHARTER.md` §8). *Needed by:* `CART.3`. `~days`
- ⬜ **`ENGINE.5` — the File device.** A cartridge picked from disk, the File System Access API where the browser has it and a file input elsewhere, read into the module's memory; no `fetch`, ever (`SD-13`). `~days`
- ⬜ **`ENGINE.6` — the accelerators, each behind the reference.** Opened only by a measurement on `CART.1` that names the bottleneck (`AD-3`'s spirit): the shared-formula vectorized path, which is `KERNEL.20`'s and lands in `vla-lang`; block memoization for a uniform synchronous rule, HashLife's table at a step of one, content-addressed; dirty regions, so an unchanged block is neither stepped nor drawn. Every accelerator follows the naive every-cell evaluation's frames, the reference, as a second implementation follows the goldens (`SD-18`'s shape); an accelerator with no oracle is not merged. `~weeks` each

## 🕹 THE CARTRIDGES

- ⬜ **`CART.1` — Life, the benchmark cartridge.** A 320 by 200 Screen whose every cell is the rule, one R1C1 shape over the previous frame's eight neighbours; a Palette sheet; a seeded soup and a glider gun as the two fixtures; `tools/check_frame_floors.ps1`, cells evaluated per second and frames per second as floors that never go down, `CHARTER.md` §3's two million a second the first bar, the honest comparison a Life that computes every generation and not HashLife (§7). It lives in this repository, since a floor's fixture lives beside the check that reads it, and ships with the engine as its demo. *Depends on:* `ENGINE.2`; `KERNEL.7`'s first functions (`SUM`, `IF`, `AND`, `OR`, the comparisons). *Oracle:* the naive evaluation is the reference, and a known pattern's generation N matches Golly's. `~days` after those
- ⬜ **`CART.2` — Sand, the first game repository.** A falling-sand toy: the mouse paints materials from a Palette sheet into a 320 by 200 grid whose rule is a downward chain, each row reading the row beneath it in this frame and the rows above it in the last (`CHARTER.md` §4, §6). Its own repository, founded when `ENGINE.3` exists, depending on `vla-lang` and `alonzo` and never on `frazaro-core` (`AD-1`'s check in its CI); its release starts the quarter of `CHARTER.md` §6's prediction. *Depends on:* `ENGINE.3`; the intra-frame graph in `vla-lang`'s step, which is `KERNEL.7`'s topological evaluation. `~weeks`
- ⬜ **`CART.3` — the falling-block game, the third.** In Tetris's shape under its own name and look (`CHARTER.md` §6): a board, a shape table, a seeded bag, keys, a score, the Audio device; its own repository. *Depends on:* `ENGINE.4`; `KERNEL.8`'s lookups. `~weeks`
- ⬜ **`CART.4` — a stacking game in Stacker's shape, the engine's second cartridge.** The owner's favourite, filed 2026-10-08. The cabinet (LAI Games, 2004): fifteen rows of lights; a row of three blocks slides side to side, faster on each row, and a button stops it; the blocks not directly above the row beneath are removed, a complete miss ends the game, the row narrows to two blocks at level four and one at level ten, eleven rows wins the minor prize and fifteen the major, with the lights spelling WIN. As cells: a Board sheet, fifteen rows by the cabinet's seven columns; a State sheet holding the row, the position, the direction, the width, the phase and the speed; the Input sheet's one button, read on its rising edge through the two buffers, pressed this frame and not last; the Clock's frame number driving a phase counter whose period falls with the row; the moving row's cells a window of the position and the width, frozen on the press by keeping only the cells above a lit cell of the row beneath, a per-cell AND, the width then the row's SUM and zero the loss; the Audio sheet a tick per move, a tone per freeze and a fanfare at the top; an Orientation cell the Screen formulas read, which turns the cabinet's upward stack into a sideways one for a wide screen, a transpose and not a rule (the owner's thought, decision 4 in the scoping). About twenty formulas and a hundred cells: the smallest arcade game there is, and the first cartridge whose rule reads the Input sheet, play rather than authoring (Life's mouse, when it has one, toggles cells of the Board and is an edit of the model, which no rule reads), so it exercises the Input, Clock and Audio devices with the least logic that is a game, before the sand toy's rule reads the Mouse. It cannot test throughput. What it says beyond itself: the cabinet's win rate is the operator's setting, about one in eight hundred at the regular one by the article's figure, through a timing window the manual describes while calling the game one of skill; on Alonzo the window and the speed are cells of a sheet anyone can read, so the only rigging possible is in the open, and the engine's determinism makes the claim of skill literally true. It lives in this repository under 0BSD as the demo of `ENGINE.3` and `ENGINE.4`, under its own name, since Stacker is LAI Games' product name. *Depends on:* `ENGINE.2`, `ENGINE.3`, `ENGINE.4`; `KERNEL.7`'s first functions (`IF`, `AND`, `OR`, `SUM`, `MOD`, the comparisons; the speed a formula of the row, so no lookup). *Oracle:* a replay golden, the Input sheet recorded frame by frame and the Board at the end compared whole, the oracle shape every cartridge with input takes after this one. *Scoped:* 2026-10-08, in `REARVIEW.md`. `~days` after `ENGINE.4`
- ⬜ **`CART.5` — Fragments, a generative ambient piece in a tracker's shape.** Filed 2026-10-08 at the owner's naming, at the approval of `SPEC.1`, after State Azure's generative modular performance (`CHARTER.md` §6, §10; `SPEC.md` §12). A Score sheet, instruments as rows and time as columns; the Audio device's four voices read under a playhead, `INDEX` over the Score's column for the beat; the playhead a lit column on the Screen, the piano roll drawn by formulas, so the visualizer is the game; the generative part as formulas of the last frame, a slow oscillator as `SIN` of the frame over its period, a sample-and-hold over the seeded dice, a clock divider, an envelope as a piecewise formula of the frames since its trigger, a quantizer over a row that holds a scale, each a VLA macro Frazaro expands into rows at build time, so a module is a macro, a patch is a sheet and a control voltage is a cell; the control log golden by the replay, the sound measured (`AD-4`). The line it draws is the frame rate: control signals that move over seconds are cells, the waveform and audio-rate modulation are the Web Audio graph's. *Pays into:* the two later versions of the page it asks for, the patch as a sheet of modules and connections the host builds the graph from, and a MIDI port as a device, in and out, where the browser has the Web MIDI API, a local device and not the network, so a keyboard plays into the Input sheet and the score plays out to a MIDI-to-CV module and a rack of real modules. It lives in this repository under 0BSD as the demo of the Audio device's reach, unless the owner founds it as a repository of its own. *Depends on:* `ENGINE.4`; `KERNEL.8`'s `INDEX` (until then `CHOOSE` over `MOD`); `KERNEL.7`'s `SIN`. *Oracle:* a replay golden whose end is the Audio sheet's log, note for note, frame for frame. *Scoped:* later, at the owner's call. `~weeks` after `ENGINE.4`
