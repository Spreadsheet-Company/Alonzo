## The standing decisions

The charter's seven rules, numbered so an item can cite them. The text is the charter's (§4), and an amendment is made there first.

- **`AD-1`. VLA's seams are the interface, and Frazaro is a door.** `alonzo` consumes `vla-lang` and never `frazaro-core`. A game's dependency tree holds the language crate and the engine crate and not the Frazaro core, checked by `cargo tree` in every game's CI. Frazaro's web door may depend on Alonzo; its crates, its command-line door, its add-in and its treaty never do.
- **`AD-2`. Every early brick is also a Frazaro item, laid where it is first needed.** The ID is Frazaro's and stays in its registers; the code lands on the side of the line `AD-4` draws.
- **`AD-3`. Nothing Frazaro rents is rebuilt until Frazaro has users on the rented version.** The measurement that says the rent is the bottleneck is named before the rebuild begins.
- **`AD-4`. Frazaro has no clock; Alonzo is the clock.** What can be a golden is VLA's or Frazaro's; what can only be measured is Alonzo's. The check on each side is the import section: zero there, an allowlist here.
- **`AD-5`. The engine's imports are the host's functions, named by hand and held to a list; the engine crate depends on the language crate and nothing else.** Each import, when one is needed, an `extern "C"` function the host shim provides, declared one by one; no generated glue, no second dependency; the first specification needs none, every device being a sheet read and written through the exports (`SPEC.md` §8.2). Adopted by the owner 2026-10-08 at the scoping of `REPO.1`; its second sentence amended the same day at the approval of `SPEC.1`.
- **`AD-6`. Everything the engine holds is a cell of a named sheet, viewable through the one view call and, except the previous frame's twins and the plane, writable through the one write call; a device is a sheet's layout in `SPEC.md`, and no device holds state that is not a cell.** Reflection over the whole stack, sanctified: the Keys sheet, the Palette, the Clock and the `.last` twins are its first fruits, and every such act is a row in the one notation, logged by the replay. Adopted by the owner 2026-10-08 at the approval of `SPEC.1`.
- **`AD-7`. The step computes values and never formulas; a formula enters the grid by `load` or by a `write` made on a person's act, and the engine's own effects write values.** Eval by a person, the REPL, kept; eval by the program refused, as the `GENSYM` veto's `eval` clause already has it; the grid writes the grid in data through the Write sheet (`SPEC.md` §3.9); held by a test, the formula cells equal before and after a step. Adopted by the owner 2026-10-08 at the approval of `SPEC.2`, the charter amended first.

Frazaro's standing decisions that cross the road are cited by their own numbers and never re-minted: `SD-13` (no outbound call, ever), `SD-18` (VBA the reference for the language), `SD-23` (one model, many projections; the interface's own state), `SD-34` (every engine interruptible, its progress a sentence), the owner's veto of `GENSYM`, which carries `eval` by its own words (`AD-7`), and the conformance treaty.

## The order, and why

Each item is what makes the next cheap, the instrument before the operation: `REPO.2` before the first import, `SPEC.1` before the first call into the language, `SPEC.2` before the four calls are written against the page, `CART.1` before any accelerator. An item that waits on Frazaro names the Frazaro item and never re-mints it. Two tracks run in parallel until the loop: the repository and the specification need nothing from Frazaro, and the renderer needs only the view record, which exists as goldens; the loop and everything after it need the `vla-lang` cut, Frazaro's work before `KERNEL.7`, which `SPEC.1` tells what to expose. The frame-rate floor needs `KERNEL.7`'s first functions. The games after the first are repositories of their own and leave this file when they are founded. The reasoning and the fan-out table are in `REARVIEW.md` under the sittings of 2026-10-08.

*Noted 2026-10-11, at the owner's word, with the filing of the kit (`KIT.1` to `KIT.4`) and `ENGINE.7`:* a cartridge is easier to build when what two cartridges write alike is written once, and the core stays a core when it never takes the shape of one game. So a pattern moves by a ratchet, each step with its proof. From the cartridges into the kit at its second appearance, the cartridges then calling it and expanding to their bytes unchanged, which proves the hoist changed no frame and leaves every replay's digest and every floor's fixture standing. From the kit into the core, a kernel, a projection, a device or a function of the language, only when a measurement or a capability asks for it and a second client stands behind it, Frazaro's recalculation floors counting as one, the kit's cells then its oracle wherever they can be one. The core learns patterns, never games. The kit's first three items come before `CART.4`, so that the first game is written in the kit's words, and `KIT.4` comes with `CART.6`.

*Noted 2026-10-12, the owner's name, with `CART.2`'s first:* **the playtest pass** follows a game's working build and its hand test. The game is played as a person plays it, by a scripted session of its own (Sand's is `engine/examples/play.rs`), every frame held to its reference and each phase timed, and in the browser beside it. Then `CART.1`'s route runs over what the session finds: a profile, each candidate prototyped in scratch and measured against the build before it, and the findings filed. A language mechanic is asked of Frazaro with its numbers, an engine or host fix lands here, a cartridge's fix is a slice of its own, and a new mechanic waits for a later pass. The floors time a game's worst stretch; the session is what found Sand's stall at every click.

As cells: a Board sheet, fifteen rows by the cabinet's seven columns; a State sheet holding the row, the position, the direction, the width, the phase and the speed; the Input sheet's one button, read on its rising edge through the two buffers, pressed this frame and not last; the Clock's frame number driving a phase counter whose period falls with the row; the moving row's cells a window of the position and the width, frozen on the press by keeping only the cells above a lit cell of the row beneath, a per-cell AND, the width then the row's SUM and zero the loss; the Audio sheet a tick per move, a tone per freeze and a fanfare at the top; an Orientation cell the Screen formulas read, which turns the cabinet's upward stack into a sideways one for a wide screen, a transpose and not a rule (the owner's thought, decision 4 in the scoping). About twenty formulas and a hundred cells: the smallest arcade game there is, and the first cartridge whose rule reads the Input sheet, play rather than authoring (Life's mouse, when it has one, toggles cells of the Board and is an edit of the model, which no rule reads), so it exercises the Input, Clock and Audio devices with the least logic that is a game, before the sand toy's rule reads the Mouse. - ⬜ **`CART.7` — chess, against a textbook opponent.** Filed 2026-10-11 at the owner's request, the second of the three, its opponent `CART.6`'s shape. Chess has the deepest printed literature and the largest rules. The rules as cells: a Board sheet of sixty-four values, with the castling rights, the en passant file, the halfmove clock and the positions since the last capture or pawn move held beside it as values, for the fifty-move rule and threefold repetition; every move a geometry allows, about 1,800 from-to pairs for the side to move with castling and the promotions, each a row whose path is the fixed squares between, wired by the pass, since a ray's squares are known at build time; check, the pins and the king's escapes read from two attack maps, all static. The opponent: an opening book of three to five lines a side, held as move sequences, matched against the game's own moves and chosen among by the seed; then every legal move scored by the primers' features, material, a piece-square table, development and castling, the centre, pawn structure, king safety, mobility, a piece left hanging by the static exchange on its square, and the named motifs, fork, pin, skewer and discovered attack, as patterns over the attack maps; then a second ply over the best few, each answered by the other side's best reply under the same evaluator, and deeper only along forced lines, checks and captures, which are narrow by nature. The cost of that second ply is what the scoping must measure: a position's analysis is thousands of cells and a full second ply is about 1,200 positions, so the beam is narrow, or the search is spread over frames, one candidate's replies a frame, its progress a sentence (`SD-34`) (*prediction*). This is Shannon's Type B program (1950) and the plausible-move generator Bernstein's program of 1958 was, seven moves a ply and four plies deep, and its history is this item's warning: the selective programs lost to the full-width ones once Chess 4.0 dropped selection in 1973, so the opponent is to be legible, a primer's player that rarely hangs a piece in one move, and not strong. The endgames the primers teach as procedures, king and rook against king first, are a later slice, Bratko's Advice Language, written in Prolog, being the precedent. *Depends on:* `CART.6`, whose opponent's shape it reuses. *Oracle:* perft, the move generator's standard test, the count of legal move sequences to each depth from the published positions (20, 400 and 8,902 from the start; the castling, en passant and promotion positions after it), held in the engine's tests to a generator written by hand and to the published counts; the book exam, a suite of a primer's positions with the primer's move, the agreement counted, a floor; a replay golden. *The decisions the scoping opens on, the owner's:* promotion to a piece other than a queen; the openings and their source; the endgame procedures; the second ply's width and the frames a move may take. `~weeks` after `CART.6`
- ⬜ **`CART.8` — go (weiqi, baduk), on nine by nine, against a textbook opponent.** Filed 2026-10-11 at the owner's request, the third of the three, its opponent `CART.6`'s shape. Nine by nine is the sitting's recommendation: the board beginners learn on, 81 points where nineteen by nineteen has 361, so the analysis of a candidate costs about a fifth of the cells and the board's candidates times their analyses about a twentieth; and the nineteen-line openings the guides teach, joseki and fuseki, do not carry over to it anyway. The rules are Tromp and Taylor's, the shortest written statement of go: area scoring, a pass, the game over on two passes in a row, and no agreement about dead stones, since play goes on until they are taken. As cells: a Board sheet of 81 values; the groups as the rules' own fixed point, each stone's label the least of its own and its like neighbours' labels, iterated, unrolled within the frame or carried across frames through the twin, Datalog's naive evaluation one round a frame (`docs/ALGEBRA.md` §6.1, the frame as an iterative solver); each group's liberties counted from the labels; a move's legality, its captures and its suicide read locally from the current board's groups, since a placed stone joins only its neighbours' groups and captures only a neighbouring group whose last liberty it fills; ko against the earlier positions, held as values, the last one alone under simple ko. The opponent: the primers' priorities, take what is in atari, save one's own, atari what cannot escape, a ladder read as the forced line it is, along its diagonal to the edge or a breaker, statically; shape by the three-by-three patterns and the proverbs (an empty triangle is bad; hane at the head of two stones); the opening by the guides' first moves, the centre, the 3-3 and 4-4 points, chosen among by the seed; territory by influence, Zobrist's function of 1969, the first go program's, or Bouzy's 5/21, five dilations then twenty-one erosions, both operations on a cell's neighbours and so a frame's natural work; and a pass when no move gains.
